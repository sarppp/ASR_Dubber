What the code and measurements establish

  1. Subtitle display timing becomes a speech constraint. German coarse translation spans are divided by word count. Those boundaries do not describe the timing of a German performance.
  2. Sentence context is broken. Current grouping uses character/time limits rather than phrase boundaries. In the benchmark, 19 of 24 chunks lack sentence-terminal punctuation. This is a heuristic, not proof every boundary is wrong.
  3. The fitting policies are inconsistent. New workers allow unlimited compression. The older fitter slows down as far as 0.65× and can trim trailing words when speech is still too long.
  4. Pauses and acoustic context change. New workers can use the entire gap to the next segment. Packaging replaces the original soundtrack with generated speech, removing background sound.
  5. Instrumentation has been too weak. Latest reports keep a rate but discard raw speech and omit text, boundaries, and measured durations. Exact before/after rerenders of those recent phrases require fresh synthesis.
  6. Translation itself sometimes sounds wrong. Text inspection found investment “compounding” rendered as “Zusammenstellung,” and “for twenty years” rendered as “in zwanzig Jahren.” These are transcript comparisons, not acoustic judgments. Five examples with surrounding English/German context.

Research treats source speech/pause structure and prosodic alignment as dubbing objectives: isochrony-aware translation and prosodic alignment. FFmpeg documentation explains tempo filtering; chaining filters avoids its >2× sample-skipping mode, but does not establish perceptual naturalness.

Candidate fix and remaining experiment

The isolated candidate groups fragments at punctuation, speaker changes, and pauses; retains original cue provenance; never slows speech to fill a slot; borrows at most 150 ms from a short same-speaker gap while retaining 100 ms; and uses an experimental 1.15× compression ceiling. Text that cannot fit is marked for intervention, not clipped. Underfilled slots are flagged rather than declared solved.

This is not a deployed fix. Punctuation is only a heuristic, estimated subtitle times still need source-pause alignment, and the 1.15× threshold must be tested by listening. The completed experiment saved native audio for both groupings. Next, investigate faithful shorter translations and better speech windows; restored background sound is a separate factor to test.

A counterfactual timing sweep confirms that a speed ceiling alone is insufficient: with current grouping and original phrase ends preserved, 54 of 56 previously compressed Mind Control phrases and 75 of 79 Bill phrases still cannot fit at 1.15×. Raw durations here are inferred from recorded multipliers; uncompressed phrases have unknown raw duration and are excluded. This is a feasibility check, not an estimate of how a new performance would sound.
